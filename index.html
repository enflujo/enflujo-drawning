I What we do

II Project history

01

Interfaces

We create interfaces for encounters with the archive. An interface can be digital, performative or developed with a community: a way of navigating films, a score that invites people to respond through movement and sound, a gathering where drawing and conversation bring images into relation with lived experience.

These interfaces shape what can happen with the archive. We build and change them as the work develops.

2015–2016

BOZAR and erg

The decisive impulse for What_if… came from a screening of footage from Belgian colonial archives at BOZAR in Brussels in 2015, organised with the Cinémathèque Royale de Belgique and the Royal Museum for Central Africa in Tervuren.

Anna Seiderer and Alexander Schellow were invited to a panel discussion. The debate became so heated that the screening itself could not take place.

This situation made palpable the continuing violence inscribed in the so-called “official” images of Belgian colonial history: a violence that remains active in the present, through verbal and physical conflict and the ongoing reproduction of colonial perspectives.

In response, Schellow and Seiderer initiated a workshop at erg—école de recherche graphique in Brussels in 2016. The workshop led to the formation of GREYZONE ZEBRA.

02

Observation and response

Observation continues through the responses of others. An image acquires different associations when it travels, when someone speaks alongside it, when a body repeats a gesture or a computer assigns an improbable shift.

What happens in these encounters becomes material for further work.

2017–2019

Colonial family films

Between 2017 and 2019, GREYZONE ZEBRA searched for colonial family films held in private collections. Digitised in high resolution, the material was gradually brought together in an online archive.

Colonial images are not merely remnants of the past. Their visual regimes remain embedded in contemporary cultural, social and perceptual practices, where they are continually reproduced, often unintentionally.

Family films are particularly revealing. Unlike official colonial productions, they often lack a coherent ideological structure or a hermetically controlled visual language. Their significance may emerge through the margins of the frame, incidental details, gestures, absences or elements that seem irrelevant at first sight.

The films can be “re-animated”: kept accessible to new forms of attention, interpretation and collective analysis, without restoring them to an imagined original meaning.

GREYZONE ZEBRA members

Milena Desse, Sandra Heremans, Maxime Jean-Baptiste, Nelson Makengo, Fred Mutombo, Alexander Schellow, Anna Seiderer and Antje Van Wichelen. The work took the form of an archive, a publication series, performances and workshops.

03

Friction and disagreement

We are interested in the capacity of artistic practice to produce meaning through these crossings, including the friction and disagreement they generate.

We embrace the archive’s contradictions and make them visible.

We notice frictions and embrace them.

We create interfaces that are warm, even if they create a fire.

Methodology

Archive activations

From its initial phase, GREYZONE ZEBRA worked with the films through formats extending beyond archival collection and presentation. “Archive Activations” were performative gatherings in which participants took notes during or immediately after screenings, then worked collectively with the resulting material.

These notes recorded observations, gestures, associations, memories and stories that emerged through the encounter with the films. Over time, the accumulated records became an independent body of material.

Workshops enabled participants to examine these observations in greater depth. Artists and researchers were invited to “process” the films in both senses of the word: technically, through editing, categorisation and transformation; and collectively, through embodied, discursive and performative analysis.

04

Archives and communities

We dislocate archives from monumental institutions and return them to communities so they can be activated.

Collaborations

Institutional collaborations

Collaboration with African partner institutions has been integral from the beginning: PICHA in Lubumbashi, the University of the Western Cape in Cape Town and the Centre for the Less Good Idea in Johannesburg.

This is more than an institutional exchange or an expansion of the archive’s geographical reach. It is a way of questioning the colonial conditions under which images were produced, preserved, circulated and interpreted, and of developing analysis beyond European archival frameworks.

Partner institutions

PICHA, Lubumbashi; University of the Western Cape, Cape Town; Centre for the Less Good Idea, Johannesburg; erg—école de recherche graphique and WIELS, Brussels; CONTOUR Biennale, Mechelen; Khiasma; Université Paris 8; FRArt/FNRS; Universidad de los Andes, Bogotá.

05

The name

The name combines drawing as an action on the archive, being drawn to it, and drowning as a risk of immersion. The brackets keep these readings present within the same word.

Play is part of how we work with that attraction and risk.

2020 onwards / What_if…

Classification and interfaces

Since 2020, Juan Camilo González, Alexander Schellow and Anna Seiderer have continued the research through What_if…, independently of the earlier GREYZONE ZEBRA collective, with the sustained support of the EnFlujo Laboratory at Universidad de los Andes in Bogotá.

The archive’s classification systems, interfaces and navigation tools actively shape how material can be encountered. Technical decisions are inseparable from questions of content, politics and ethics. The structure of access influences whether an archive is rigid or playful, closed or open to future uses.

González works at the intersection of animation and creative programming. Together, we investigate categorisation systems and their playful, critical deconstruction. The technical research has also involved Hugo Idarraga.

Recognition, misdirection and the politics of access

Since 2020, experiments with non-linguistic pattern recognition and artificial intelligence have raised questions about categorisation, automation, dataset construction and the political implications of machine-assisted navigation.

Performative play arrangements bring found or intentionally altered deep-learning processes and datasets into resonance with colonial family films. Misplaced, anachronistic or deliberately manipulated patterns expose the assumptions, biases and historical structures embedded in systems of recognition.

How might algorithmic processes be interrupted, misdirected or made perceptible? Can machine-learning systems become sites of friction in which categorisation itself is subjected to artistic and political scrutiny?

Initial workshops in Brussels, Bogotá and Johannesburg took the form of performative scores, bringing together animation, drawing, music, performance and programming.

The project also addresses tensions between open-source models, archival accessibility and the right to one’s own image.

Archive activations ↗

Collective screenings and workshops using participants’ notes, drawings and gestures as research material.

Workshop description

Developed during the GREYZONE ZEBRA phase, these performative gatherings invite participants to record and share the observations, gestures, memories and stories that emerge alongside a screening.

In workshops, this material is worked through collectively, allowing individual perceptions to become the beginning of further artistic research.

Drawing · Conversation · Handmade books

Draw[n]ing images ↗

Drawing and bookmaking workshops with families experiencing exile.

Workshop description

What images drive and punctuate our journeys? What images stick with us, bind and animate us, for better or for worse?

How can images, voices and gestures intertwine, challenge and subvert colonial and xenophobic clichés while telling other stories of migration?

“The journey I looked forward” invites families facing experiences of exile to shape memories through handmade books. The workshop text brings together Brussels (erg), Johannesburg (Centre for the Less Good Idea) and Lubumbashi (PICHA).
